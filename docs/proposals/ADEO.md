# Brief for ADEO

Focus: usability for a policy team. Project: Taadeel (تعديل), a research and navigation tool for amendments to UAE federal legislation. It is not legal advice. The official Arabic text published by the competent authority prevails.

## Problem

A policy analyst who asks what changed in a law since a given date has to find every amending instrument, read each one, and reconstruct the current text by hand. The answer depends on effective dates that are often stated in a different document from the amendment itself. Errors are easy to make and hard to see. A tool for this work must show what it knows, what it does not know, and where each statement comes from.

## What exists

* A web page and an API that answer "what did this law say on date X" and "what changed between two versions", with article level diffs shown as a side by side redline.
* Provenance on every provision: a link to the official source and a retrieval date.
* Three visible states for a point in time answer: certain, uncertain with candidate texts, and not in force.
* Distinct badges for synthetic data, UK validation data and real data, so that test material cannot be mistaken for law.
* A UK validation corpus (legislation.gov.uk, Open Government Licence v3.0) that policy users can search to try the workflow on real English text, with the licence attribution shown.
* A live demo at https://taadeel.onrender.com. It uses synthetic data for the UAE view and may take up to a minute to wake up.

## Workflow: what changed since date X

1. Choose the view. Synthetic is the default for the demonstration; the real view states plainly that no real UAE data is loaded.
2. Choose the work and set the as of date with the date field or the slider.
3. Read the state shown for that date: certain, uncertain, or not in force. An uncertain answer lists the candidate texts instead of picking one.
4. Open the changes view to see the redline between versions. Each change is classed as inserted, replaced, repealed, renumbered or clarified, and a low confidence match is flagged, not forced.
5. Follow the source link on any provision to the official publication, and check the retrieval date.
6. Record the finding with its source and date. The official Arabic text remains the reference.

## Measured evidence

Scope is stated on each line. None of it concerns UAE law.

* **Uncertainty is surfaced, UK English, 7 Acts.** Of 80 questions about the day before a change, a method that used recorded effects only served the wrong text for 17. The snapshot aware method answered certain and correct on 56, certain and wrong on 3, and uncertain on 21 (26.2%). In 18 of the uncertain answers the true text was among the candidates shown; in 3 it was not. It was the first candidate in 7 of the uncertain answers. The pre registered checks held on 5 of 5 (T0 hit, T1 hit, T2 hit, T3 hit, T4 hit).
* **Point in time before this change, UK English.** The earlier method was correct on 63 of 80 day before queries (78.8%); 17 were wrong because a change took effect on a date the recorded effects did not give.
* **Amendment wording found by pattern, UK English, second sample.** 81 of 198 recorded effects found (40.9%), precision lower bound 87.5%. Where the wording sits in a schedule, recall was 39.5%. This feature is a navigation aid and misses many amendments.

## Uncertainty display

* Certain: no change between the recorded dates is left unexplained by the recorded effects.
* Uncertain with candidates: a change occurred at a date the recorded effects do not give, or an effect conflicts with the snapshots. The page shows the candidate texts and does not choose.
* Not in force: the provision was not in force on the date asked.

The web page currently uses the effects based method for these states. The snapshot aware method measured above runs offline (`src/taadeel/timeline_v2.py`) and is not yet wired into the page.

## What is NOT validated

* Nothing is validated on UAE law or on real Arabic legal text. The demo uses synthetic data for the UAE view.
* UK numbers say nothing about UAE law. Schedules are excluded from the UK evaluation.
* Hand checks were done by the author, not by a lawyer. No usability study with policy staff has been run.
* Effective date extraction from Arabic text is not built or measured.
